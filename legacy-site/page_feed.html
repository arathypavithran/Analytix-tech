You are active on social media. However, your sales are not increasing. This is a common sign of revenue leakage; money slipping away through the cracks in your marketing efforts. The reason is usually a lack of coordination in marketing, rather than insufficient budget.  

This article explains the common reasons your marketing might be counterproductive and what you can do to fix it.  

What Does Revenue Leakage Actually Mean? 

Revenue leakage happens when your business misses out on income it should have earned. In marketing, this usually happens when there are gaps between your tools, teams, and channels. Your SEO team generates traffic. However, if the landing page fails to convert, that traffic is wasted. Your ads generate clicks. But if the CRM isn’t connected to your marketing automation system, leads will slip away and revenue will be lost.  

Research conducted by McKinsey indicates that companies can lose up to 5% of their annual income due to poor alignment between sales and marketing. For a mid-sized B2B business, this is a significant amount, and it adds up every quarter.  

The Problem: A Disconnected Marketing Stack 

Many businesses hire separate vendors, one for SEO, another for web design, another for social media, and another for ads. Each agency may fulfill its role. However, if they don’t work together, you end up with a disconnected marketing stack. The result is marketing silos, conflicting messages, and a leaky sales funnel.  

This is what costs you when you hire separate SEO and web design agencies that lack strategic alignment. You may see high website traffic but low sales, a clear sign of a marketing disconnect.  

Here’s what usually happens in a dysfunctional marketing environment: 

Signs Your Marketing Is Working Against Itself 

Ask yourself these questions:  

  • You have website traffic but no sales, and you can’t figure out why 
  • Your branding doesn’t align with your website experience 
  • Your marketing tech stack consolidation has never been done; each tool operates separately  
  • When results do not meet expectations, vendors start pointing fingers at each other 
  • You can’t determine which channel brought in the last sale 

If any of these sounds familiar to you, the problem isn’t your budget. It’s poor coordination and absence of a single point of accountability in your marketing activities.  

The Fix: An Integrated Marketing Approach 

Consider digital marketing as a wheel. SEO, content, design, ads, and social media are the parts that keep it moving. If one part is missing or malfunctioning, progress slows down. Integrated marketing brings every part together under one strategy.  

A full-service marketing agency manages the whole customer journey, from awareness to conversion to retention. This means your SEO strategy guides your content, your content supports your ads, and your ads connect to a CRM that tracks every lead. This enables effective revenue attribution.  

The four key areas that must work together:  

Foundation: Your website, branding, and tech stack. This is where everything starts. Poor UX or inconsistent branding destroys trust even before a visitor reads your offer.  

Acquisition: SEO, ads, and social media. These channels are effective only when conveying the same message and targeting the same audience.  

Conversion: Landing pages, CTAs, and lead qualification. Even high traffic can be wasted without a clear path to conversion. Conversion Rate Optimization (CRO) helps bridge this gap.  

Retention: Email, CRM, and nurturing. Most revenue leakage happens here, when qualified leads do not get follow-up. Improving CRM data to improved retention is one of the quickest ways to recover lost revenue.  

Revenue Operations: The Glue That Holds It Together 

Revenue Operations (RevOps) involves coordinating your sales, marketing, and service teams with shared data and goals. When your Marketing Technology Stack (MarTech) connects CRM, analytics, automation, and reporting, you can finally determine what truly drives revenue.  

Tools like GA4 assist in tracking attribution leaks across your funnel. However, having tools alone isn’t sufficient. The solution lies in marketing alignment, where every team and every tool are working toward the same goal.   

A tech stack audit is a valuable first step. It allows you to identify where leads are dropping off, where messaging is unclear, and where you are investing without seeing results.  

How Analytix Technology Supports This 

We work with businesses to help them adopt technology that improves their efficiency. Whether it’s building a connected digital marketing ecosystem, using Odoo ERP for better visibility, or implementing AI process automation to minimize manual errors, our goal is to eliminate obstacles in your business processes.  

When it comes to marketing, we help businesses transition from separate marketing channels to a unified strategy, where design, web development, SEO, and digital marketing work together. This is how you can prevent losses and start to see significant outcomes.  

In Summary  

If your marketing isn’t bringing in revenue, the solution is usually not to spend more. Instead, it’s often due to poor marketing coordination, a disconnect between your channels, teams, and tools. To fix this, start with a clear audit, a single point of accountability, and an integrated marketing services model where all elements of your marketing ecosystem work together. That’s how businesses stop losing revenue they should already be generating.

]]> 4975 How Aramco Cybersecurity Standards Protect the Local Economy  https://analytixbss.com/technew/how-aramco-cybersecurity-standards-protect-the-local-economy/ Tue, 21 Apr 2026 11:28:09 +0000 https://analytixbss.com/technew/?p=4972 The Ripple Effect: Why One Vendor’s Security Affects Everyone 

When a single supplier in Saudi Aramco’s network experiences a cyberattack, the effect rarely stops there. Contracts get delayed, deliveries are hindered, and partners are put under pressure. In an interconnected supply chain, one vulnerable link can drag others down too. This is exactly why Aramco cybersecurity standards exist: to protect not just one company, but the whole economic ecosystem.  

The Cybersecurity Compliance Certificate (CCC) and the SACS-002 standard are the two pillars of this system. Together, they define the requirements for how vendors should secure their systems before they can work with Aramco. Adhering to these standards isn’t just a formality; it represents a commitment to Digital Economy Resilience and the sustainable growth of Saudi Arabia’s economy.  

This article explains the requirements of these standards, their significance to local businesses, and how compliance helps achieve Vision 2030’s goals for a secure and prosperous digital future. If you are a supplier or business owner looking to expand in Saudi Arabia’s energy sector, this is a must-read.  

What Is Aramco Cybersecurity Compliance? 

The CCC and SACS-002 Standard 

The Cybersecurity CCC, or Cybersecurity Compliance Certificate, is a mandatory credential for all third-party vendors working with Saudi Aramco. To obtain this certificate, companies need to fulfill the criteria set by SACS-002, which is Aramco’s cybersecurity framework that includes 17 control categories for both IT and OT (operational technology) environments.  

The TPCSS, Third Party Cybersecurity Standard, serves as the governance framework for evaluating and certifying vendors. It works alongside the NCA Framework from the National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA) and is in line with PDPL (the Personal Data Protection Law), which regulates the management of sensitive data by businesses in Saudi Arabia.  

This means when you pursue Aramco CCC certification, you are also aligning with national-level security policy, strengthening your position not only with Aramco, but across the Saudi market.  

Who Needs CCC Certification? 

SACS-002 focuses on vendors with direct or indirect access to Aramco’s systems, data, or facilities. The standard classifies them into five main categories:  

If your business fits into any of these categories, the CCC is mandatory. Without it, you won’t be able to qualify for Aramco tenders or contracts. 

Key Security Controls: What SACS-002 Requires 

The SACS-002 compliance framework includes 17 control categories, ranging from access control to incident management. Below are the core technical controls that every vendor needs to implement:  

These are more than mere technical requirements. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) secures the login credentials your employees use daily. Data Loss Prevention (DLP) prevents your business data from being leaked, whether on purpose or by mistake. Encryption (AES-256) helps ensure that even if data is intercepted, it remains unreadable. Penetration Testing and Vulnerability Management find weaknesses in your systems before they can be exploited by attackers.  

Security Audit Logs and Incident Reporting work together to provide your business and clients with a clear view of your systems at all times, plus a structured plan for when issues arise.  

How Compliance Protects the Local Economy 

Supply Chain Security and Business Continuity 

Aramco’s supply chain includes thousands of local vendors. Supply Chain Security standards help ensure that if one company suffers a breach, it does not affect the entire network. This protects Business Continuity for all businesses linked to the chain.  

Consider it this way: if a local logistics vendor is compromised and operational data is stolen, Aramco’s procurement schedules might be affected. That disruption has a cost, and it usually impacts the local economy first. Standards like SACS-002 avoid this by mandating that every link in the chain meets a basic security standard.  

Vendor Risk Management and Critical Infrastructure Protection 

Effective Vendor Risk Management is a direct outcome of CCC compliance. When vendors are evaluated and certified, Aramco can trust them. That trust brings real value: continued contracts, long-term partnerships, and opportunities for bigger projects.  

On a national scale, these standards help protect Critical Infrastructure Protection, ensuring the safety of energy systems, pipelines, and digital networks that support Saudi Arabia’s economy. The National Cybersecurity Authority (NCA) has prioritized this under Vision 2030, which seeks to diversify the Kingdom’s economy and build a strong digital foundation.  

Data Residency and Regulatory Sanctions Avoidance 

Under the PDPL, businesses must ensure Data Residency, which means keeping personal and operational data within approved boundaries. SACS-002 strengthens this rule. Vendors that overlook it may face Regulatory Sanctions Avoidance challenges that could completely stop their operations.  

The Business Case for CCC Certification 

Contract Eligibility, Market Credibility, and Business Expansion 

The immediate advantage of certification is Contract Eligibility, as it allows your business to legally bid on Aramco tenders. However, the long-term benefits are even greater. Market Credibility increases when clients recognize that you adhere to Aramco’s standards. This shows that you prioritize security, which builds trust with both government and private sector clients.  

This credibility supports business growth. Many international companies entering Saudi Arabia need their local partners to hold recognized security certifications. CCC compliance makes your business a qualified, trusted partner in the Kingdom’s expanding digital economy. 

IKTVA Compliance and Localization 

Saudi Aramco’s Localization (IKTVA) program, In-Kingdom Total Value Add (IKTVA) – incentivizes suppliers that develop local talent, resources, and capabilities. IKTVA Compliance along with CCC certification strengthens local businesses’ competitive edge for Aramco contracts, directly aiding the localization goals of Vision 2030.  

Operational Stability, Reputational Protection, and Incident Response 

Beyond contracts, CCC compliance ensures Operational Stability. Businesses with strong security controls are less likely to suffer expensive downtime. Reputational Protection comes next; a breach that leaks client data does long-term damage. When incidents happen, a certified Incident Response Capability allows your team to respond effectively, reducing damage and recovery time.  

How to Obtain Aramco CCC Certification 

The process requires a systematic approach:  

  1. Gap Assessment: Review your current security posture against SACS-002 requirements.  
  1. Remediation: Apply required controls (MFA, DLP, Encryption, etc.) across IT and OT systems.  
  1. Documentation: Build your evidence pack: security policies, audit logs, and test reports. 
  1. Audit: A certified assessor examines your systems and documentation.  
  1. Certification: After passing, you receive the CCC and can qualify for Aramco tenders.  

The timeline differs depending on the company’s size and readiness, but generally, businesses should expect to take several months from assessment to certification. Starting the gap assessment early allows you to resolve issues without missing out on tender opportunities.  

Conclusion: Security Is a Foundation, Not a Formality 

The NCA Framework, SACS-002, and the Cybersecurity Compliance Certificate are not just technical requirements. They form the basis of a secure, competitive, and resilient economy in Saudi Arabia. 

When local suppliers adhere to these standards, they protect their own businesses. They also protect the supply chains, data, and digital infrastructure that the whole Kingdom depends on. This is how Digital Economy Resilience is built, with each certified vendor supporting the goals of Saudi Vision 2030.  

Achieving compliance brings clear benefits to businesses. Contract Eligibility, Market Credibility, and Business Expansion are not just concepts, they are real opportunities for any Saudi business willing to invest in security.

]]> 4972 Why the Future of Tech is Powered by Humans https://analytixbss.com/technew/why-the-future-of-tech-is-powered-by-humans/ Tue, 21 Apr 2026 09:09:03 +0000 https://analytixbss.com/technew/?p=4853 Artificial intelligence is changing how we create software, run businesses, and make decisions. It generates code, automates processes, and analyzes data faster than any person can. This raises a natural question: are humans still important in the future?  

The answer is yes, and it is more important than any companies realize. Technology does not advance by itself. People do. In this article, you will learn why humans are still important in the tech future, what roles they will take on with AI, and how to get your business ready for this change.  

Can AI Replace Humans? 

This is one of the most frequently asked questions, and the worry is valid. AI tools already generate a significant share of code, with estimates suggesting that almost half of all new code has some AI involvement. Some tech leaders have predicted that machines might be able to write most code independently by 2040.  

But here is the key part: the World Economic Forum’s Future of Jobs Report 2025 indicates that software and AI-related roles are growing the fastest, with forecasts of hundreds of thousands of new software engineering jobs coming up.  

AI effectively manages repetitive tasks, such as writing standard code blocks or flagging basic errors. However, it lacks the creativity, problem-solving ability, and specialized knowledge that human developers provide. A person is still needed to determine what to build, how it integrates into the overall system, and if it actually solves a real problem. That person will always be human.  

But to answer the question “Will AI replace coders by 2040?”, the answer is maybe for some entry-level tasks. However, McKinsey predicts that around 80% of programming jobs are expected to remain human-centric. The nature of the job will change, but it won’t disappear.  

Is Digital Transformation Really a People Problem? 

A fact that surprises many business leaders is that 70% of digital transformation initiatives do not meet their objectives, and most of these failures are due to issues related to people rather than the technology itself.  

Poor software adoption, resistance to change, and lack of training often lead to more harm than any technical flaw. McKinsey’s Ricky Asemota stated, “To truly transform, we need to go beyond the tech and really think about purpose and people.” 

This tells us something significant: software, automation, and AI are only as good as the people using them. A company that invests in the best software development tools but ignores its team’s skills is headed for failure.  

The most important skills that will matter going forward are judgment, communication, adaptability, and leadership. These are all human skills.  

What Do Experts Say? 

Artificial intelligence is great at speed and recognizing patterns. However, it finds it difficult to grasp context, nuance, and understanding how the real world works.  

AI models learn from data, but they cannot connect information to the physical world, which results in a lack of common sense and understanding of causality. This is where human thinking matters.  

This is exactly where human judgment is needed. Questions such as “Will this decision affect our customers’ trust?” or “Does this process make sense for our team?”, need real-life experience and empathy. Artificial intelligence cannot provide reliable answers to these questions.  

If you’re wondering ‘What roles will humans play in AI development by 2030?’; the answer is that the World Economic Forum predicts that by 2030, 39% of job skills will transform, requiring technical talent to have a combination of AI fluency, systems thinking, and human judgment. People will guide, validate, and enhance what AI produces. The most valuable roles will combine technical understanding with human insight. 

# Skill Relevance Why it matters & how to develop it
1 Critical judgment Essential AI produces outputs but cannot evaluate whether they are right for your context. Develop this by questioning AI results, stress-testing decisions, and practising structured reasoning.
2 Creative thinking Essential AI recombines existing patterns; humans generate genuinely new ideas. Strengthen this by working across disciplines, encouraging open-ended problem-solving, and giving teams room to experiment.
3 Empathy Essential Understanding how a decision affects real people is something AI cannot replicate. Build this through active listening, customer interaction, and leadership that centres human experience.
4 Adaptability Very high Technology changes fast. The ability to learn new tools, shift approaches, and embrace change is what separates high performers. Practise by regularly stepping outside familiar workflows.
5 Communication Very high Getting people to understand, trust, and act on a decision is a human job. Develop this through cross-team collaboration, clear writing, and regularly explaining technical ideas to non-technical audiences.
6 AI fluency Very high Knowing how to work with AI tools, what to trust, what to question, and how to get the best results, is becoming a baseline skill. Start with hands-on use of AI tools in your current role.
7 Systems thinking High Seeing how one change affects the whole organisation is a skill AI lacks. Build it by mapping processes end-to-end, involving multiple stakeholders in decisions, and studying how past changes rippled through your business.
7 Ethical reasoning High As AI takes on more tasks, humans must ask whether its outputs are fair, safe, and responsible. Develop this by building ethics reviews into projects and creating space for teams to raise concerns without fear.

What This Means for Your Business 

The table above illustrates the existing split between AI and human capabilities. It is clear that in every aspect, the human role revolves around judgment, creativity, and context. These aspects cannot be automated.  

There are a few clear strategies individuals and businesses can start getting ready. Those who successfully adopt AI tend to share certain traits: they combine AI speed with human judgment, invest in training their teams, implement quality checks on AI-generated outputs, and use AI for repetitive tasks, so people can focus on innovation.  

If your business is considering new technology, like an ERP system, AI process automation, or a new digital platform, the most important investment you should make is in your people. Guide them to understand the tools, help them feel confident in using them, and show them the benefits of the change.  

Will Technology Replace Humans in the Future? 

Not in a complete way. Technology will keep transforming the nature of work. Some tasks will be handled by machines. New kinds of work will arise. Instead of a complete elimination of jobs, we are witnessing a change in the nature of work, with AI generating new roles, particularly in areas like systems design and applied machine learning.  

The companies that succeed will be those that treat technology as a tool to enhance human capabilities, rather than a substitute for human thinking.  

Generative AI is boosting human capacity in some very practical ways. In software development, AI-assisted code generation allows developers to spend less time on repetitive tasks and more time on tackling engaging challenges. This increases the potential of what a small, skilled team can accomplish. It’s a human benefit, made possible by technology.  

How to Help Businesses Make the Shift 

Technology adoption is more than just a technical challenge. It’s a business challenge. Understanding which tools to use, how to implement them effectively, and how to guide your team through the process demands skill and experience.  

We work with companies to do just that. Whether it’s implementing Odoo ERP to simplify operations, creating AI-driven process automation, ensuring cybersecurity compliance, or building digital solutions from scratch, the emphasis is always on making technology work for its users. The goal is not to replace human effort but to improve its efficiency.  

In Summary  

The future of tech isn’t about choosing between humans and machines. It’s about creating the right mix of both. Artificial intelligence and automation are powerful tools. But they work best when skilled and experienced people are directing them.  

Invest in your team’s ability to use new technology. Be careful about the tools you choose and why. Also, remember that innovation doesn’t start with purchasing software. It starts with people who understand the issue and are ready to solve it. This has never changed, and it will remain true in 2030 and beyond.
